Platform Analysis

Business School Websites

Business school websites show the most official and controlled version of a school’s marketing message. Unlike Instagram and LinkedIn, websites are not built around a feed. They work more like a home base where schools organize their program identity, admissions information, rankings, curriculum, career outcomes, network, flexibility, and return on investment.

Official Program Story

Websites present the school’s most polished version of itself, including program descriptions, degree options, rankings, curriculum, and admissions details.

Career and Value

Many business school websites emphasize career outcomes, employer connections, alumni networks, leadership development, and the value of the degree.

Student Experience

A strong website should also help students imagine the experience by showing culture, belonging, support, practical learning, and flexibility.

Website Experience

What should a business school website make visible?

A business school website should do more than list facts. It should help a prospective student understand the practical details while also imagining success, belonging, and opportunity.

Website Standouts

Which websites stood out the most?

To make the website comparison more visual, I highlighted a few schools that stood out for design, navigation, program identity, and overall impression. These are not formal rankings, but observations from reviewing the websites as a prospective student experience.

Best Design

Most Visually Attractive

Northwestern Kellogg

Kellogg felt the most polished and premium from a visual branding perspective. The website made the school feel professional, established, and confident.

Best Identity

Clearest Program Identity

Michigan Ross

Ross stood out because its action-based learning message was easy to understand. The practical experience theme gave the website a clear and memorable identity.

Best Navigation

Easiest to Navigate

Northwestern Kellogg

Kellogg felt organized and easy to move through. The site made it simple to find information about careers, community, and program value.

Strongest ROI

Strongest Career and ROI Message

SMU Cox and UT Austin McCombs

These websites made career outcomes, employer connections, and value feel especially visible. Their messaging strongly connected the degree to professional opportunity.

Strongest Culture

Strongest Culture and Belonging Message

Duke Fuqua, Notre Dame Mendoza, and Baylor Hankamer

These websites made the school feel like more than a business degree. Their messaging emphasized values, community, purpose, and the kind of environment students would join.

Best Fit

Most Likely to Make Me Feel Successful

TCU Neeley and Northwestern Kellogg

TCU made success feel personal and attainable, while Kellogg made success feel prestigious and expansive. Together, they show two different ways a website can create confidence.

Website takeaway

The website comparison shows that business school websites function as the official “proof” layer of each school’s marketing. Instagram often builds emotion, visibility, and belonging, while LinkedIn highlights professional achievement and credibility. Business school websites provide the most complete program story by organizing the brand around outcomes, rankings, curriculum, career support, alumni networks, practical experience, logistics, flexibility, and return on investment.
