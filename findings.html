Project Findings

What the Platform Comparison Shows

This project found that universities and business schools do not tell the same story everywhere online. Instagram, LinkedIn, and business school websites each play a different role in shaping how schools communicate identity, credibility, value, and opportunity.

Main Finding

Schools tell different stories depending on the platform.

Across the project, Instagram, LinkedIn, and business school websites each served a different marketing purpose. Instagram was more visual and emotional, LinkedIn was more professional and achievement-focused, and business school websites worked as the official proof layer where schools explained outcomes, rankings, curriculum, career value, and return on investment.

A simple way to summarize the project is this: Instagram sells the feeling, LinkedIn sells the credibility, and websites prove the value.

Instagram

Instagram helped schools create emotion, visibility, personality, community, student life, and belonging.

LinkedIn

LinkedIn helped schools communicate professional credibility, alumni success, rankings, achievement, and employer connections.

Websites

Business school websites provided the official program story through outcomes, academics, rankings, logistics, flexibility, and ROI.

Finding One

Instagram builds identity, emotion, and belonging.

The Instagram data shows how schools use visual storytelling to create a sense of campus identity and connection. General university Instagram accounts often highlight school spirit, student life, events, and community. Business school Instagram accounts still use visual storytelling, but they tend to connect the school brand more directly to programs, careers, alumni, admissions, and professional value.

This comparison matters because it shows that Instagram is not used the same way by every type of account. A general university account often speaks to a broad school community, while a business school account speaks to a more specific academic and professional audience.

Finding Two

LinkedIn emphasizes credibility and professional achievement.

LinkedIn worked differently from Instagram. Instead of focusing mainly on visual identity or student life, LinkedIn posts were more connected to professional reputation, rankings, leadership, employer relationships, alumni success, and career outcomes.

The LinkedIn comments added another layer because they showed how audiences responded in a public professional space. Many responses were brief, supportive, and achievement-focused, which fits the platform’s professional tone.

Finding Three

Business school websites act as the official proof layer.

The website comparison showed that business school websites are the most controlled version of each school’s marketing message. Unlike Instagram and LinkedIn, websites are not organized around a feed. They are designed to give prospective students the full program story.

The strongest websites made important decision factors easy to see, including career outcomes, ROI, network strength, culture, academic quality, practical experience, logistics, flexibility, belonging, and opportunity. The website page showed that effective business school marketing needs both information and feeling.

Finding Four

Broader business school accounts showed stronger average engagement.

One question that came out of the data was whether account structure affected engagement. Some schools used a broader School of Business account, while others used a dedicated MBA or graduate programs account. To compare these account types, I calculated average total engagement per post by adding likes and comments together.

Account types used in this comparison

For this engagement comparison, I grouped the business school Instagram accounts into two account structure categories.

  • School of Business pages: Duke Fuqua, Michigan Ross, Notre Dame Mendoza, and SMU Cox.
  • Dedicated MBA or Graduate Programs pages: Baylor Hankamer Graduate Programs, Northwestern Kellogg School of Management, Vanderbilt Owen Graduate Programs, TCU Neeley Graduate Programs, Georgia Terry Graduate Programs, and UT Austin McCombs MBA.

In this sample, School of Business pages had higher average total engagement per post than dedicated MBA or Graduate Programs pages. This suggests that broader business school accounts may benefit from stronger visibility, clearer naming, and a larger existing audience.

While dedicated graduate accounts can speak more directly to MBA or graduate students, they may also be harder for prospective students to find. Many prospective students may naturally search for the main business school page first because they do not know what a side account is called. If graduate program information is shared only on a smaller side account, it may reach a more targeted audience, but it may also miss people who begin with the main school brand.

Finding Five

Higher rankings did not automatically explain Instagram engagement.

I also compared U.S. News business school rankings with average Instagram engagement to see whether higher-ranked schools automatically received more interaction. The chart suggests that rankings may provide some context, but they do not fully explain engagement.

Some highly ranked schools, such as Northwestern, Michigan, Duke, and UT Austin, showed strong average engagement. However, the pattern was not perfect. Notre Dame also showed strong engagement despite being ranked lower than several schools in the sample, while Georgia had lower engagement even though it was not one of the lowest-ranked schools.

This comparison should also be read alongside the account structure finding. Some schools used broader School of Business Instagram accounts, while others used smaller MBA or graduate programs accounts. Those smaller dedicated accounts may receive less engagement because they are harder to find, have narrower audiences, or do not benefit from the same built-in visibility as the main business school page.

Overall, the ranking comparison suggests that Instagram engagement is shaped by more than prestige alone. Account visibility, audience size, content style, school identity, and account structure may all influence how much interaction a school receives.

Final Takeaway

Digital marketing is about more than content. It is also about structure and discoverability.

Overall, the project shows that higher education marketing changes depending on the platform, the audience, and the account structure. Instagram helps schools create emotion and identity. LinkedIn helps schools build professional credibility. Websites provide the official evidence behind the brand. The engagement comparisons add another important point: even strong content may perform differently depending on where it is posted and how easy that account is to find.

For business schools, the strongest digital strategy may be one that connects all three layers: emotional storytelling on Instagram, professional credibility on LinkedIn, and clear proof of value on the website, while also making sure important graduate program information is visible from the main business school brand.
